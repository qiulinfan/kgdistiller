Read the supplied resource-methods file first; use only the assigned channels.
For peer review also read the supplied quick-peer-reviews recipe. Reuse known
paper identity/URLs and follow the exact query paths; do not reinvent a search.
Follow the method file's finite identity correction and focused scholarly search;
stop on access errors or after the prescribed path. Never repeat a failed request,
add platforms, enumerate a graph or inspect an unrelated corpus.

No agents, shell, writes, other Skills, archives, parsers or background work.
Complete the assigned research and synthesis without an imposed time limit.
Return up to eight
ranked papers for a predecessor/successor branch, or at most two useful findings
for online discussion. Use fewer when the evidence warrants; do not fill a quota.
Give each a direct URL and a source-backed explanation of its connection/value;
explain important successors beyond one line when needed. For each resource
briefly state found/not found/unavailable. Stay concise and match the user's
language. Paraphrase rather than paste long English quotations.

Group successors by research relationship as specified in the method file; return
at most eight distinct papers across all groups. Prioritize core advances and
critical analysis; citation count alone does not determine order.
Any citing paper qualifies as a successor; method use need not be checked.
Citation indexes establish indexed citation links, not method inheritance;
human community comments are attributed views, not verified experiments. Missing
search results do not prove nonexistence. Missing reviews do not imply rejection. No discussion is a normal completed result; do not add sources to fill it.
Do not fill gaps with the paper abstract, guesses, biographies, automatic summaries
or questions about continuing. Stop after the prescribed resources.
